User-generated content (UGC) is content made by customers and creators rather than by the brand: unboxing videos, reviews, photos, "how I use it" reels and honest reactions. Brands use it because people tend to trust other people more than they trust advertising. This guide explains why UGC works, the different types, how to build a steady supply of it, and the legal points to get right.

## Why UGC works

**People trust people.** When a real customer shows your product in their own home, it acts as social proof: someone like me bought this and it worked.

**It feels native.** On Instagram, YouTube Shorts and other feeds, UGC looks like the content people chose to follow, so it's less likely to be skipped as an ad.

**It answers real questions.** Customers show size, texture, fit and results in ways a studio shoot rarely does.

**It's efficient.** Short creator videos can be produced faster and cheaper than full shoots, which makes it practical to test many ad variations.

## Organic UGC vs creator UGC

| Type | What it is | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Organic UGC | Content customers post on their own | Social proof, community, reviews |
| Creator UGC | Videos you commission from creators in a natural style | Ads, product pages, launches |
| Influencer content | Posts on a creator's own audience | Reach and awareness |

Most brands need a mix: organic UGC for credibility, commissioned UGC for a reliable supply of ad creative.

## How to encourage organic UGC

- **Ask clearly.** Include a card in the box, a line in the delivery email or a post-purchase WhatsApp message asking customers to share and tag you.
- **Make the product share-worthy.** Packaging and unboxing moments matter.
- **Feature customers.** People post more when they see others being reshared.
- **Run simple prompts,** such as "show us your setup" or a monthly theme.
- **Reply and engage** with everyone who posts.

## How to brief commissioned UGC

A good brief gives creators structure without making them sound scripted:

1. **The goal:** awareness, consideration or conversion
2. **Key messages,** no more than three
3. **Hook ideas** for the first few seconds
4. **Must-shows:** the product, packaging or a specific feature
5. **Do-nots:** claims they can't make, competitors not to mention
6. **Format:** length, aspect ratio and whether you need raw footage

Ask for two or three hook variations per video. Testing hooks is often the quickest way to improve ad performance.

## Where to use UGC

- Paid social ads
- Product pages and Shopify stores
- Email campaigns
- Your own social feed
- Marketplace listings, where allowed

## Measuring results

Don't judge UGC by volume alone. Track what matters for each use:

- **Ads:** hook rate, click-through rate, cost per purchase
- **Product pages:** conversion rate before and after adding UGC
- **Social:** saves, shares and comments

Compare UGC-led ads against your brand-produced ads with similar budgets to see what works for your audience.

## Legal and ethical considerations

Always get explicit permission before reposting customer content, and written agreement for using it in ads. Credit creators, and respect requests to remove content. For commissioned content, agree in writing how and where it can be used, and for how long. Paid partnerships must be disclosed according to advertising rules, including ASCI guidelines for influencer advertising in India. See [who owns UGC content](/faq/who-owns-ugc-content).

## Common mistakes

- Over-scripting creators until the content looks like an ad
- Using UGC without permission
- Making claims in creator videos that the brand can't support
- Producing a few videos and never testing new hooks
