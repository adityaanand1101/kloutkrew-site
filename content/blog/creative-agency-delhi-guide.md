"Creative agency" covers a lot of ground. Some agencies only design logos; others plan TV campaigns or run your Meta ads. Choosing the wrong type means paying for skills you don't need, or discovering mid-project that the agency can't deliver what you actually needed. This guide explains how the main types of agencies differ, and how to pick the right one for your stage.

## Agency types at a glance

| Type | Focus | Typical outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Branding agency | Identity and strategy | Naming, logo, guidelines, packaging |
| Creative agency | Ideas and content | Campaigns, design, films, social |
| Ad agency | Advertising campaigns | TV, print, digital ads, media plans |
| Digital agency | Online channels | Websites, SEO, paid media, social |
| Production house | Making films | Shoots, editing, post-production |

## Branding agency

A branding agency defines how your business looks, sounds and feels: strategy, naming, logo, colours, typography, packaging and guidelines. You need one when you're launching, rebranding, or when your brand looks inconsistent across channels. The output is a system others can use, not a campaign. See [brand identity design](/branding-agency).

## Creative agency

A creative agency turns your brand into ideas and content: campaign concepts, social content, design, films and launch assets. It's the right choice when your brand exists and you need a steady flow of good work in the market. Many creative agencies also offer branding, which helps keep everything consistent.

## Advertising agency

A traditional ad agency builds campaigns for mass media such as TV, print, outdoor and digital, often with a separate media-buying team that negotiates placements. It suits larger brands with significant media budgets running national campaigns.

## Digital and performance agency

A digital agency focuses on online channels: websites, SEO, paid search and social ads, analytics and conversion. Performance agencies are measured on results such as leads, sales and return on ad spend. They're essential for online sales, but many rely on clients or other agencies to supply the creative.

## Production house

A production house makes films: pre-production, shoots, editing and post-production. It usually works from someone else's script and concept, either yours or your creative agency's. AI-led production studios are a newer option for faster, lower-cost films; see [AI brand films](/ai-brand-films).

## Which do you need?

- **Launching a new brand:** branding first, then creative and digital
- **Growing sales online:** digital and creative
- **Big awareness campaigns:** creative or ad agency
- **Need everything consistent:** an integrated studio

## Integrated studio or specialists?

**Specialists** go deep in one area, which suits larger brands with an in-house marketing team to coordinate them. **An integrated studio** covers several disciplines, such as branding, packaging, website, content and ads, with one team. For startups and D2C brands, an integrated team usually means fewer handovers, faster decisions and a more consistent brand, because the people who designed the identity also make the ads.

## Questions to ask any agency

- Which part of the work do you do in-house, and what do you outsource?
- Who will work on my account day to day?
- How do you measure success for a project like mine?
- Can I see work you've done for a brand at my stage?
