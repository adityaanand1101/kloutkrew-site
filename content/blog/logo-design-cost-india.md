"How much does a logo cost?" has no single answer in India. You can pay nothing for a logo-maker template or several lakhs for an agency-led identity, and both can be the right choice depending on what you're building. This guide explains the market ranges in 2026, what actually drives the price, and how to decide what level is right for your business.

## Typical ranges in 2026

| Option | Typical range | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Logo makers and beginner designers | ₹0 – ₹1,000 | Templates, little originality |
| Logo templates | ₹500 – ₹6,000 | Pre-made, not unique |
| Design contests | ₹2,000 – ₹10,000 | Many quick options, little strategy |
| Basic professional logo | ₹5,000 – ₹20,000 | Original mark, limited revisions |
| Agency brand identity | ₹1,00,000+ for larger businesses | Strategy, full system, guidelines |

Globally, [ManyPixels](https://www.manypixels.co/blog/brand-design/logo-design-cost-guide) puts most small-business logos at $300–$2,000.

## What you're actually paying for

A logo looks like a small file, but most of the cost is in the thinking and checking behind it.

- **Research.** Understanding your customers, your price point and what competitors look like, so your mark doesn't blend in. A logo for a premium skincare brand and one for a budget snack brand need very different personalities.
- **Exploration.** Good designers sketch dozens of directions before showing two or three. Cheap options skip this and go straight to a template.
- **Refinement.** Adjusting spacing, curves and letterforms so the logo holds up at 16 pixels and on a shop sign.
- **Versions and files.** A professional logo comes as a family: primary, horizontal and stacked lockups, an icon, one-colour versions and vector files.
- **Experience.** A designer who has seen their logos printed on packaging, embroidered on uniforms and shrunk into app icons avoids mistakes you'd only discover later.

## What each price level is good for

**Under ₹1,000 (logo makers):** fine for testing an idea for a few weeks, a college project or an internal tool. The risk is that thousands of businesses can use the same template, and you usually can't trademark it.

**₹5,000–₹20,000 (freelancer or small studio):** a reasonable starting point for a local business or an early-stage startup. Ask to see the designer's process, not just their portfolio, and confirm you'll get vector files and full ownership.

**₹1,00,000 and above (agency identity):** right when the logo will be printed on packaging, used across a website and ads, or presented to investors. At this level you're buying a system: colours, type, imagery and guidelines, not just a mark.

## What a professional logo should include

- Original design you own outright once paid
- Primary, secondary and icon versions
- Colour, black and white versions
- Vector files (SVG, PDF, EPS) and PNGs. See [logo file formats](/blog/logo-file-formats-explained)
- A short usage sheet: clear space, minimum size, colours and what not to do

## Hidden costs to plan for

- **Trademark search and filing.** Budget for a trademark attorney before you print anything. Rebranding after a trademark dispute costs far more than the logo did.
- **Fonts.** If your logo or brand uses a commercial typeface, you may need a licence for your team and website.
- **Applications.** Business cards, signage, packaging and social templates are usually priced separately from the logo itself.
- **Revisions beyond scope.** Agree upfront how many rounds are included.

## How to get the most from your budget

1. **Write a clear brief.** What you sell, to whom, at what price, and three competitors. Our [logo design brief guide](/blog/logo-design-brief) shows what to include.
2. **Ask to see concepts on real applications,** not floating on a white background. You'll judge them more accurately.
3. **Decide who decides.** Gather input early, then let one person approve. Logos designed by committee tend to end up safe and forgettable.
4. **Think one step ahead.** If you'll need packaging or a website within a year, it's often cheaper to design the identity system now than to retrofit one later.
