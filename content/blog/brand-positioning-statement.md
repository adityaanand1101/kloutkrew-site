A **brand positioning statement** is a short, internal sentence that keeps every decision on track, from your logo colour to your Instagram captions.

## The formula

> For **[target customer]** who **[need or problem]**, **[brand]** is the **[category]** that **[key benefit]**, because **[reason to believe]**.

## Examples

**D2C skincare:** For working women in Indian metros who want simple routines, Brand X is the skincare range that gives visible results in three steps, because every formula is built around one active ingredient.

**B2B service:** For architecture firms that win work through their portfolio, Studio Y is the web partner that turns projects into enquiries, because every site is image-first and built to load fast on phones.

**Food brand:** For snackers who read labels, Brand Z is the chip that tastes indulgent with a short ingredient list, because it's baked in small batches with five ingredients.

## How to test yours

| Test | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Specific | Could a competitor say exactly the same thing? |
| Relevant | Does the customer actually care about this benefit? |
| Believable | Is there proof? |
| Useful | Does it help you choose between two design options? |

## Using it in design

Positioning decides visual choices. A "premium and precise" brand points to restrained colour and tight typography. A "joyful and accessible" brand points to bright colour and rounded shapes.
