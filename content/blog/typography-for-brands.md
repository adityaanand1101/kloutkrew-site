Typography carries a brand's voice before anyone reads a word. A condensed bold sans feels loud and modern; a refined serif feels editorial and premium.

## What brand type needs to do

- Express personality in headlines
- Stay readable in long text and on small screens
- Work in print, on packaging, on websites and in video
- Support the languages you use

## A simple pairing approach

| Role | Look for |
|---|---|
| Display | Character, works big, distinctive |
| Body | Readable at small sizes, good on screens |
| Optional mono or accent | Data, labels or a specific tone |

Pair fonts that contrast clearly (for example a condensed display face with a wide, open body face) rather than two similar ones.

## Licensing

Fonts are software with licences. Desktop, web, app, packaging and broadcast use can be licensed separately. Free fonts from reputable libraries usually allow commercial use, but check.

## Setting type on the web

- Use WOFF2 files and preload the main font
- Limit weights to what you actually use
- Set comfortable line length and line height

On our own site, moving to optimised WOFF2 fonts cut one font from 239 KB to 76 KB. Small details like this matter for [website speed](/blog/core-web-vitals-explained).
