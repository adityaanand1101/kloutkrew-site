Two brands can sell the same product with the same design and still feel completely different because of how they write. That's **brand voice**.

## Define three traits

Pick three adjectives, then describe what each is and isn't.

| Trait | We are | We are not |
|---|---|---|
| Warm | Friendly, plain-spoken | Over-familiar, slangy |
| Confident | Direct, specific | Arrogant, pushy |
| Playful | Light, witty | Silly, sarcastic |

## Show before-and-after examples

**Generic:** "We offer high-quality skincare solutions for all skin types."
**On-voice:** "Three steps. Clean ingredients. Skin that looks like you slept eight hours."

## Build a word list

- Words we use (e.g. "routine", "real ingredients")
- Words we avoid (e.g. "revolutionary", "best-in-class")
- How we write numbers, dates, prices and emojis

## Adjust tone by situation

Social posts can be lighter; refunds, delays and complaints need calm, clear language in the same voice.

## Put it in the guidelines

Your voice belongs in your [brand guidelines](/blog/brand-guidelines-what-to-include), next to logo and colour. We define voice as part of every [brand identity](/branding-agency).
