Colour is often the first thing people remember about a brand, before the name or logo. Here's how to choose **brand colours** that are distinctive, flexible and practical.

## 1. Map your category

Collect logos, packs and websites from ten competitors. Most categories cluster: green in organic food, blue in finance, black and gold in luxury. Owning a colour that competitors don't use is one of the fastest ways to be recognised.

## 2. Match your positioning

Bright, saturated colours feel energetic and accessible. Muted tones feel calm and premium. Dark palettes feel serious. Choose what matches your [positioning statement](/blog/brand-positioning-statement).

## 3. Build a full palette

| Role | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Primary | Your signature colour, used boldly |
| Secondary | Support and variety for campaigns and products |
| Accent | Calls to action and highlights |
| Neutrals | Text, backgrounds, packaging details |

## 4. Test contrast and accessibility

Text must be readable. Check contrast ratios for text on your main backgrounds; aim for at least 4.5:1 for body text.

## 5. Test in the real world

- On a phone in sunlight
- Printed on matte and gloss stock
- As a thumbnail next to competitors
- In dark mode

## 6. Write the values down

Record HEX and RGB for screens, CMYK for print and Pantone if you print packaging. Put them in your [brand guidelines](/blog/brand-guidelines-what-to-include).
