A **brand identity** is the set of visual and verbal tools that make a company recognisable: logo, colours, typefaces, imagery, tone of voice and the rules that hold them together. Here's how a professional brand identity project works, what each stage is for and what you should have at the end.

## Before you start: what you need

- A clear idea of what you sell, to whom and at what price.
- Two or three competitors you're up against.
- Brands you admire, in or outside your category.
- Where the brand will live first: website, packaging, social, a pitch deck.
- A launch date, if you have one.

## Step 1: Brief

The brief turns your business goals into design goals. A good studio will ask about your customers, your price point, what makes you different, and what the brand has to do in the next twelve months. The output is a short written brief you both agree on.

## Step 2: Research and strategy

The studio looks at your category: what competitors look like, which colours and styles are overused, where there's space to stand out. This becomes a short strategy, usually a positioning line, brand personality and a few design principles that every later decision is checked against.

## Step 3: Concepts

The studio presents **two or three distinct directions**, each shown on real applications such as a website header, a pack, a social post and a business card. Seeing a logo in context is the only way to judge it properly. At KloutKrew Studio, first concepts arrive within 7 days of the kickoff call.

## Step 4: Refinement

You choose one direction, and the studio refines it: the logo is redrawn for precision, colours are tested on screen and in print, and typefaces are paired and licensed.

## Step 5: Building the system

The chosen direction is extended into a full identity:

| Element | What's created |
|---|---|
| Logo suite | Primary logo, secondary lockups, a small mark for avatars and favicons |
| Colour | Primary and secondary palettes with HEX, RGB and CMYK values |
| Typography | Headline and body typefaces, sizes and spacing rules |
| Imagery | Photography or illustration style, with examples |
| Graphic elements | Patterns, icons, shapes and layout grids |
| Voice | Tone-of-voice principles and sample copy |

## Step 6: Guidelines and handover

Everything is documented in **brand guidelines** your team, printers and freelancers can follow, and every source file is handed over. Good studios also deliver launch assets, such as social templates, a deck template or packaging artwork.

## How long does it take?

| Stage | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Brief and research | Week 1 |
| First concepts | Within 7 days of kickoff |
| Refinement | Weeks 2 to 3 |
| System and guidelines | Weeks 3 to 5 |
| Handover | Weeks 4 to 6 |

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing a logo on its own,** without seeing it on a pack, website or social post.
- **Designing by committee.** Agree who decides before concepts are shown.
- **Skipping guidelines,** so the brand drifts within months.
- **Branding after launch,** when changing packaging, signage and the website costs far more.
