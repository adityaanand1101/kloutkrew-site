A name is the one part of your brand you'll say thousands of times. It should be easy to say, hard to confuse and possible to own. Here's a practical process for **brand naming**.

## The main naming styles

| Style | Example pattern | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Descriptive | "Delhi Packaging Co." | Instantly clear | Hard to trademark, generic |
| Suggestive | Hints at a benefit | Memorable, ownable | Needs some explaining |
| Invented | A new word | Most distinctive | Needs marketing to give it meaning |
| Founder or place | A person or place name | Feels personal or rooted | Can limit growth |
| Compound | Two words joined | Flexible | Can feel generic if overused |

## A step-by-step process

1. **Write a naming brief.** What should the name suggest? Who must understand it? Which markets?
2. **Generate widely.** Aim for 100 or more options across several styles before judging any.
3. **Shortlist ten.** Remove anything hard to spell, pronounce or remember.
4. **Say it out loud.** Over the phone, in a noisy room, in Hindi and English.
5. **Check meanings.** In every language spoken in your target markets.
6. **Check availability.** Domains, social handles and a trademark search in your classes.
7. **Test with real people.** Ask what they think the business does.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a name only because the domain is free.
- Using trendy spellings that customers mistype.
- Ignoring trademark checks until after printing packaging.
- Picking a name so specific you outgrow it in a year.
