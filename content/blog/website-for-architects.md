For an architecture practice, the website is the portfolio, the first meeting and the reference check all at once. Clients, developers and competition juries look at it before they ever call. Yet many **architect websites** are slow, hard to navigate on a phone and impossible to find on Google. Here's what a good one needs.

## What makes an architect's website different?

An architecture website is **image-led, project-led and trust-led**. The work has to look as good as it does in print, each project has to tell a short story, and the site has to make a serious client confident enough to get in touch. That means large photography, careful typography and a structure built around projects rather than marketing copy.

## The pages every architecture website needs

| Page | Its job | Must include |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Make a first impression in five seconds | One hero project, a one-line statement of what you do and where, a path to the portfolio |
| Projects | Let visitors browse your work | Filters by type (residential, commercial, interiors), location and year |
| Project page | Tell the story of one project | Photos, drawings, location, size, year, brief and your approach |
| About / Studio | Build trust in the people | Founders, team, philosophy, awards and publications |
| Services | Explain what you can be hired for | Architecture, interiors, masterplanning, consulting |
| Contact | Turn interest into an enquiry | A short form, phone, email, office location and map |

## Five features that win enquiries

1. **Project pages that read like case studies.** A short brief, the challenge and your response, not just a gallery.
2. **Fast, full-screen images.** Modern formats like AVIF and WebP keep big photos sharp without slow loading.
3. **Mobile-first layouts.** Most first visits come from phones, often from a link shared on WhatsApp or Instagram.
4. **A visible enquiry path.** A contact button on every page and a form that asks only what you need.
5. **Easy updates.** Your team should be able to add a project without calling a developer.

## Speed: why it matters more for architects

Architecture sites are heavy by nature, with dozens of large photographs per project. Without optimisation, pages take five to ten seconds to load on a phone, and visitors leave. Ask your web designer to compress images into modern formats, serve them at the right size for each screen, lazy-load images below the fold, and test with Google Lighthouse before launch.

## SEO basics for architecture firms

- **One page per project** with a descriptive title, such as "Courtyard house, Gurugram | Studio Name".
- **Descriptive image alt text**, like "double-height living room with timber screen", which helps Google Images and accessibility.
- **A Google Business Profile** with your office, photos and reviews.
- **Location and building-type pages** if you want to rank for searches like "residential architect in Delhi".
- **Occasional articles** answering client questions, for example costs, timelines or approvals.

## Which platform should you use?

Webflow is our usual recommendation for architecture firms: it handles image-heavy, editorial layouts well, loads fast and lets your team add projects through a simple editor. WordPress is an option if you need a specific plugin. For the full trade-offs, read our [Webflow vs Framer vs WordPress comparison](/blog/webflow-vs-framer-vs-wordpress).

## Checklist

- Best project on the home page
- Filterable project portfolio
- Every project on its own page with a brief and credits
- Team and studio story
- Contact button on every page
- Images in AVIF/WebP, lazy-loaded
- Mobile layout tested on a real phone
- Google Business Profile set up
- Lighthouse performance checked
