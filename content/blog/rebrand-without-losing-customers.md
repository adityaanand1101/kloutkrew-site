A rebrand should bring new customers without confusing the ones you have. Here's how to roll one out.

## 1. Keep some equity

Before changing everything, list what customers recognise: a colour, a shape, a mascot, a name. Keeping one or two of these helps people connect old and new.

## 2. Prepare every touchpoint

| Touchpoint | Check |
|---|---|
| Website and favicon | New logo, colours, social share images |
| Social profiles | Avatars, covers, highlight icons, bios |
| Google Business Profile | Logo, photos, name if changed |
| Marketplaces | Brand store, listing images |
| Packaging | Artwork, phased by stock |
| Email and documents | Signatures, invoices, decks |

## 3. Protect search visibility

If your name or domain changes, set up permanent (301 or 308) redirects from every old URL, update Search Console, and update listings on directories like Clutch and Google Business Profile.

## 4. Tell the story

Announce the change with a short explanation: what's new, why it matters to customers, what's staying the same. A short brand film or reveal post works well.

## 5. Phase physical items sensibly

Don't destroy usable stock. Phase new packaging in, and make sure both versions clearly belong to one brand.

## 6. Measure

Watch branded search, direct traffic, social mentions and customer feedback for three months.
