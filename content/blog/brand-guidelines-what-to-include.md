**Brand guidelines** are the rulebook for your brand. They explain how to use your logo, colours, type, imagery and voice so that a social post made by an intern, a box printed in Ahmedabad and a website built in Bengaluru all look like the same company.

## Why brand guidelines matter

Without guidelines, a brand drifts. Someone stretches the logo, another person picks a slightly different red, a freelancer adds a third typeface. Each change is small, but after a year the brand looks inconsistent, and customers trust inconsistent brands less. Guidelines also save time: decisions are made once, not re-argued on every project.

## What to include

| Section | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Brand story | Purpose, positioning and personality in a page |
| Logo | Primary and secondary versions, clear space, minimum size, what not to do |
| Colour | Primary and secondary palette with HEX, RGB and CMYK values |
| Typography | Headline and body typefaces, sizes, weights and fallbacks |
| Imagery | Photo or illustration style, with do and don't examples |
| Graphic elements | Patterns, icons, shapes and grids |
| Tone of voice | How the brand speaks, with sample sentences |
| Applications | Real examples: social posts, packaging, website, stationery |

## Make it usable

- **Show, don't just tell.** Every rule needs an example.
- **Include "don'ts".** A page of misused logos prevents more mistakes than paragraphs of text.
- **Share the files with the guide.** Link logo files, fonts and templates right inside it.
- **Keep it living.** Update it when you add a product line or a new channel.

## Checklist

- Logo versions and clear-space rules
- Colour values for screen and print
- Typefaces with licences sorted
- Imagery examples
- Tone-of-voice examples
- Social and packaging templates
- One owner responsible for updates
