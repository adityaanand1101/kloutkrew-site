FMCG packs live in some of the toughest environments in retail: crowded kirana shelves, modern trade aisles and quick-commerce grids. **FMCG packaging design** is about being seen and chosen in seconds, at scale, across many products, sizes and price points, and then being produced reliably in very large print runs. This guide covers what makes FMCG packaging work in India and how to plan a range that can grow.

## Three environments, one pack

| Where | What matters |
|---|---|
| Kirana stores | Bold brand block, readable from a distance, hanging strips and sachets |
| Modern trade | Shelf blocking across a range, clear variant coding |
| Apps | Thumbnail legibility, front-facing pack shots |

A pack that only works in one of these places leaves sales on the table. Test every design in all three: from two metres away, lined up with competitors on a shelf, and as a small image on a phone.

## Win the first glance

In FMCG, shoppers rarely read. They recognise. The most valuable thing a pack can have is a **distinctive brand block**, a shape, colour and logo arrangement people can spot instantly. Protect it across every product, and resist the temptation to change it with each redesign.

## Build a range system

FMCG brands add SKUs constantly. Design a system with fixed brand and layout zones and a flexible zone for variant colour, flavour cue and pack size, so new products can be added quickly and consistently.

A good range system includes:

- **A layout grid** that works across pack shapes, from sachets to large jars
- **A colour logic** for variants, with room for future additions
- **Typography rules** for product names, claims and back-of-pack text
- **Artwork templates** so new SKUs can be produced without starting over

## Design for production

- Standardise dielines across sizes
- Plan for the print process (flexo, gravure, offset) from the start
- Limit special finishes to where they matter
- Leave clear space for batch coding and barcodes

Each print process has limits on fine detail, colour matching and gradients. Designing within those limits from the first concept avoids expensive compromises later. Always review physical proofs before a full run.

## Pricing and pack size cues

Price points and pack sizes drive FMCG decisions. Make quantity and value packs instantly clear. Many Indian shoppers buy small packs and sachets at low price points, so the smallest pack needs to carry the brand as clearly as the largest.

## Mandatory information

Food and personal-care FMCG packs must carry regulated information such as ingredients, net quantity, MRP, dates and manufacturer details, and food packs must follow FSSAI labelling rules. Plan space for these from the start, especially on small packs.

## Regional considerations

India's languages and preferences vary by region. Some brands adapt packs with regional languages or local cues; a flexible system makes that much easier.

## Redesigning an existing FMCG brand

Change carefully. Loyal customers find your pack by memory. Keep the strongest recognisable elements, usually colour and brand block, and modernise the rest. Many brands change in stages rather than all at once.

## Choosing an agency

Look for experience with ranges and large print runs, not just single beautiful packs. Ask how they handle artwork for production and how they'd add new SKUs later.
