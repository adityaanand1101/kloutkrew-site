Before a box is a box, it's a flat sheet. The **dieline** is the map of that sheet.

## What's on a dieline

| Line or area | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cut lines | Where the material is cut |
| Fold or crease lines | Where it bends |
| Glue areas | Where panels are stuck together |
| Bleed | Artwork extends past cut lines to avoid white edges |
| Safe zone | Keep important text inside it |

## Why it matters

Artwork is placed on the dieline so each panel lands correctly when folded. A logo designed for the "front" can end up upside down or on a flap if the dieline is misread.

## Getting it right

1. Get the dieline from your printer before design starts.
2. Confirm material thickness; it affects folds.
3. Mock up a physical sample by printing and folding.
4. Keep the dieline on a separate, non-printing layer.
5. Approve a printed proof before the full run.
