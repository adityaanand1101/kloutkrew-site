A **rebrand** is expensive and disruptive, so it should solve a real problem. Here are the signs it's time, and how to tell whether you need a light refresh or a full change.

## 1. Your business has outgrown the brand

You started as a single product and now sell a range, or you started local and now sell nationally or abroad. The brand built for the old business feels too small.

## 2. You look like everyone else

Put your logo, packaging and website next to your three closest competitors. If a customer couldn't tell them apart, you're competing on price alone.

## 3. Your audience has changed

A brand made for students may not convince the corporate buyers you now sell to.

## 4. The brand is inconsistent

Different logos on your website, packaging and social media, or five shades of your "brand colour". This is often fixable with a refresh and proper [brand guidelines](/blog/brand-guidelines-what-to-include).

## 5. It doesn't work on small screens

Logos designed for print often fail as app icons, favicons and marketplace thumbnails.

## 6. You're about to raise money or launch big

Investors, retailers and partners judge quickly. A dated brand can undersell a strong business.

## 7. A merger, new name or legal issue

Sometimes a rebrand is forced by trademark conflicts or a change in ownership.

## 8. Your team is embarrassed to share it

If people avoid handing out your business card, take that seriously.

## Refresh or rebrand?

| Situation | Usually needs |
|---|---|
| Looks dated, strategy still right | Refresh |
| Inconsistent use across channels | Refresh plus guidelines |
| New audience or market | Rebrand |
| New name or merger | Rebrand |
