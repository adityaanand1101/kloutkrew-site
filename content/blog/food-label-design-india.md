A food label has two jobs: sell the product in a few seconds and give buyers the information they need. Good **food label design** does both without clutter.

## Front of pack: three things only

1. **Brand** — recognisable at a glance
2. **Product name and variant** — what is it, which flavour
3. **One key claim** — the reason to buy

Everything else can move to the back or side.

## Back of pack: a consistent grid

Group mandatory information in a tidy panel: ingredients, nutrition table, allergens, net quantity, MRP, manufacturer and licence details, batch and dates. Keep positions consistent across your range so customers learn where to look.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Too many claims on the front | Choose one hero claim |
| Tiny or low-contrast mandatory text | Respect minimum sizes and contrast |
| Veg/non-veg mark hard to find | Place it clearly near the product name |
| Different layouts per variant | One grid for the whole range |
| No space for batch printing | Leave a clear area for inkjet coding |

## Test before print

Print at actual size, check at arm's length, and photograph the pack as a marketplace thumbnail.

*This is design guidance, not regulatory advice. Check current FSSAI labelling requirements for your category.*
